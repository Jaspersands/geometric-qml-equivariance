SU(2)-equivariant circuits versus barren plateaus

This page runs a statevector simulator (up to 8 qubits) in JavaScript. It samples real gradients of ⟨H⟩ for an exchange-generated equivariant ansatz and a hardware-efficient ansatz at depth L = N, verifies that the equivariant state never leaves the total-spin-0 sector, and computes Dynamical Lie Algebra closures by iterated commutators — the same computations as the Python package.

How this page computes its numbers
  • State. Complex amplitudes for 2ᴺ basis states (qubit 0 = most significant bit). Gates act in place: exp(−iφP) = cos φ − i sin φ·P for two-qubit Paulis, RY/RZ/CNOT/H for the hardware-efficient ansatz.
  • Ansätze. EQNN: |singlet⟩^{⊗N/2} then per layer one shared angle θ_l on every bond, exp(−iθ(XX+YY+ZZ)). HEA: RY·RZ on every qubit, CNOT chain, repeated L times plus a final rotation layer.
  • Gradients. Central differences with h = 10⁻⁴ on the exact expectation value (error ~10⁻⁸); sampled over uniform parameters in [0, 2π). Variance is over draws; the mean over parameters is plotted and fitted as log₂ Var = a·N + b.
  • ⟨S²⟩. S² = 3N/4 + ½ Σ_{i<j} ⟨σ_i·σ_j⟩ from the same Pauli expectation routine.
  • DLA. Generators are made traceless, mapped to iG, and closed under commutators with Gram–Schmidt in the Hilbert–Schmidt inner product until nothing new appears. The closed form Σ_S (m_S²−1)+1 uses m_S = C(N, N/2−S) − C(N, N/2−S−1).
  • Limits. Up to 8 qubits and depth 8 to stay under a few seconds; the Python package runs the same study with PennyLane backprop and larger N.
