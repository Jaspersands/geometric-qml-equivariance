Symmetric circuits and barren plateaus

A variational circuit built only from exchange gates commutes with total spin, so it can never leave the spin sector it starts in. That constraint shrinks the space the circuit explores, measured by its dynamical Lie algebra, and it changes how quickly gradients vanish as the system grows. This page simulates both kinds of circuit on up to eight qubits and measures those effects directly.

A snake ordering (row 0 left to right, row 1 right to left) puts every chain bond on a physical coupler. Each exchange gate compiles to FSim(2θ, 0) followed by CNOT·Rz(2θ)·CNOT.

Method
  • State. Complex amplitudes for 2ᴺ basis states, qubit 0 the most significant bit. Two-qubit Pauli rotations are applied as exp(−iφP) = cos φ − i sin φ·P; the hardware-efficient circuit uses RY, RZ and CNOT.
  • Circuits. Equivariant: a product of singlets, then per layer one shared angle on every bond, exp(−iθ(XX + YY + ZZ)). Hardware-efficient: RY·RZ on every qubit and a CNOT chain, repeated L times, then a final rotation layer.
  • Gradients. Central differences (h = 10⁻⁴) of the exact expectation value, over parameters drawn uniformly from [0, 2π). The variance across draws, averaged over parameters, is fitted as log₂ Var = aN + b. The sampling runs in a Web Worker.
  • Total spin. ⟨S²⟩ = 3N/4 + ½ Σi<j ⟨σᵢ·σⱼ⟩.
  • Lie algebra. Generators are made traceless and multiplied by i. Because the algebra is spanned by nested commutators [g₁, [g₂, … gk]], each round commutes the generators with the elements added in the previous round and orthonormalises the results (Hilbert–Schmidt inner product) until nothing new appears. For exchange circuits the dimension equals ΣS(mS² − 1) + 1 with mS = C(N, N/2 − S) − C(N, N/2 − S − 1), the multiplicity of total spin S.
  • Scope. Eight qubits keeps every study to a few seconds. The Python package runs the same study with PennyLane, closes the algebra up to N = 8 in a symmetry-reduced representation, and includes the kagome lattice and the Fermi–Hubbard model.
